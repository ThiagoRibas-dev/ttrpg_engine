# Turn 137 — AI-Designer

OD-001A — Shared Spell-Slot Progression
Consolidated Design Summary
1. Spellcasting Progression Level

Every character has a derived value called Spellcasting Progression Level.

This value determines the character’s position on the shared daily Spell Slot table.

It is separate from:

Character Level.
High Fantasy Tier.
Tradition Competency.
Spell Circle.
Essence.
Ability scores.

A full Wizard whose spellcasting advances every level has:

text
Character Level 1  → Spellcasting Progression Level 1
Character Level 2  → Spellcasting Progression Level 2
Character Level 3  → Spellcasting Progression Level 3
...
Character Level 20 → Spellcasting Progression Level 20

A multiclass character may have a lower Spellcasting Progression Level than their Character Level.

2. High Fantasy Tiers and Spellcasting Progression

The High Fantasy Tiers describe the character’s overall scope. They do not create separate spell-slot systems.

High Fantasy Tier	Character Levels	Full-Caster Progression	Spell-Circle Access
Trained	1–4	Progression Levels 1–4	1st and 2nd
Veteran	5–8	Progression Levels 5–8	3rd and 4th
Master	9–12	Progression Levels 9–12	5th and 6th
Hero	13–16	Progression Levels 13–16	7th and 8th
Legend	17–20	Progression Levels 17–20	9th
Mythic	21+	Epic advancement	Epic Traditions and Spells

For example:

text
Level 1 Wizard:
  Progression Level 1
  4 first-circle slots

Level 4 Wizard:
  Progression Level 4
  4 first-circle slots
  2 second-circle slots

Level 5 Wizard:
  Progression Level 5
  5 first-circle slots
  2 second-circle slots
  2 third-circle slots

This progression is more generous than D&D 3.5e because:

Ability scores do not grant bonus spell slots.
Spell access is handled by Traditions.
Spell scaling is handled by Tradition Competency.
High Fantasy characters should have meaningful magical resources early.
4. Class and Prestige Class Advancement

Classes and Prestige Classes show their Spellcasting Advancement level by level in their class tables.

They should not merely say:

text
Advances Spellcasting every 2 levels.

Instead, each level explicitly shows whether it grants an advancement.

Prestige Classes

Prestige Classes follow exactly the same procedure as Base Classes.

Their difference is that they have entry requirements.

Examples:

text
Archmage:
  +1 Spellcasting Advancement at every class level.

Eldritch Knight:
  +1 Spellcasting Advancement at selected class levels.

Death Knight:
  +1 Spellcasting Advancement at selected class levels.

A Prestige Class may also grant:

Tradition access.
Tradition Competency advancement.
Spell acquisition.
Essence abilities.
Metamagic.
Special spell permissions.
5. Multiclassing

Every class contributes its own completed Spellcasting Advancements to one shared total.

Wizard 4 / Fighter 4
text
Wizard: 4 advancements
Fighter: 0 advancements

Spellcasting Progression Level: 4

The character uses the Progression Level 4 slot row.

Wizard 4 / Paladin 4
text
Wizard: 4 advancements
Paladin: 2 advancements

Spellcasting Progression Level: 6

The character uses the Progression Level 6 slot row.

Wizard 4 / Eldritch Knight 3
text
Wizard: 4 advancements
Eldritch Knight: 1 advancement

Spellcasting Progression Level: 5

The character uses the Progression Level 5 row.

Wizard 4 / Cleric 4
text
Wizard: 4 advancements
Cleric: 4 advancements

Spellcasting Progression Level: 8

The character uses the Progression Level 8 row.

There is one shared spell-slot pool, not separate Wizard, Cleric, or Tradition pools.

6. Tradition Competency and Spell Slots

Tradition Competency does not limit the character’s Spellcasting Progression Level or available slot circles.

Instead:

text
Spellcasting Progression Level:
  Determines daily spell-slot volume.

Tradition Competency:
  Determines spell access and intrinsic spell scaling.

Spell Circle:
  Determines the spell’s slot cost and baseline complexity.

Example:

text
Spellcasting Progression Level: 10
Death: Trained
Arcane: Expert

The character receives the full Progression Level 10 slot pool, but:

Death spells use Trained-scale effects.
Arcane spells use Expert-scale effects.
The character cannot access spells requiring deeper Death Competency.
The character may spend high-circle slots on lower-circle spells, subject to spell rules.

This allows a character to have high daily magical capacity but shallow investment in a particular Tradition.

7. Spell Scaling

Spells automatically improve according to the caster’s relevant Tradition Competency.

text
Death: Trained:
  Use the Trained version of a Death spell.

Death: Expert:
  Use the Expert version.

Death: Master:
  Use the Master version.

Using a higher-circle slot does not automatically improve a spell.

A spell may have an explicit Heightened version, but that must be written into the spell entry.

8. Advanced Classes and Prestige Classes

Advanced classes may increase any combination of:

Spellcasting Progression.
Tradition access.
Tradition Competency.
Spell acquisition.
Essence capacity.
Metamagic.
Special spell permissions.

Increasing Spellcasting Progression does not automatically improve Tradition Competency.

A Prestige Class might therefore be:

text
Archmage:
  Advances Spellcasting every level.
  Grants Arcane and Rune access.

or:

text
Death Adept:
  Improves Death Competency.
  Grants Death spells and necromantic feats.
  Does not necessarily advance Spellcasting Progression.
9. Mythic Advancement

Mythic advancement does not automatically add new ordinary spell slots beyond the Level 20 table.

Instead, Mythic advancement grants:

Epic Traditions.
Epic Spells.
Mythic versions of existing spells.
Cosmic or divine magical permissions.
Access to advanced Traditions such as Creation, Time, Space, Order, Chaos, or Void.

Epic Spells use the existing shared slot pool, potentially with:

High-circle slot requirements.
Essence costs.
Ritual requirements.
Rare components.
Special locations.
Divine, cosmic, or narrative conditions.

Mythic advancement expands what magic can do, rather than simply adding more ordinary daily slots.

Final OD-001A Model
text
Classes and Prestige Classes:
  Show level-by-level Spellcasting Advancements.

Spellcasting Advancement:
  A discrete +1 entry granted by a class table.

Spellcasting Progression Level:
  The character’s total accumulated advancements.

Shared Spell Slot Table:
  Determined by Spellcasting Progression Level.

Tradition Skills:
  Determine spell access and intrinsic spell scaling.

Spell Circles:
  Determine spell complexity and slot cost.

Multiclassing:
  Add all completed Spellcasting Advancements from all classes.

Essence:
  Fuels 0th-circle spells, metamagic, casting stability,
  and supernatural class features.

Mythic:
  Grants Epic Traditions and Spells using the existing slot framework.

The remaining OD-001A implementation work is now limited to:

Designing the reduced Mediocre and Bad slot tables.
Defining exactly how 0th-circle spells use Essence.
Defining higher-slot use and explicit Heightening.
Testing the slot quantities through play and simulation.
Designing the Mythic/Epic spell framework.
