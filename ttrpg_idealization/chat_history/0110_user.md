# Turn 110 — User

Actually, let's simplify things.

Spell slots are a shared pool. There's no casting method distinction like spontaneous vs prepared casters. We use D&amp;D 5e's Vancian casting (prepare spells, slots are a resource by rank) and D&amp;D 3.5e Spell mechanics where Spells automatically get better with Caster Level (Tradition Skill for us) and casting lower level spells using higher level slots doesn't change the spell.

Classes don't inherently have spell lists of their own. They simply gate access to the Tradition Skills. A Tradition has its own fixed spell list regardless of source. (death from Cleric is the same Death from wizard).

A character preparation methods, spell acquisition methods, etc, apply to all Traditions it has invested in. So a character that has levels in both wizard and Cleric can prepare and acquire spells using the mechanisms for Wizard and Cleric as it seems fit. For a Wizard Cleric, when getting a level of Wizard it might learn 2 spells, whereas it learns 5 Spells when taking a level of Cleric, but Wizard has an acquisition mechanic where it can learn spells from research or scrolls or whatever.

That allows players to diversify in mechanics or traditions, sacrificing one or the other. A pure wizard might have a single spell acquisition method but 4 traditions maxed out, while a Cleric Wizard might have two Traditions maxed out (the ones common between both classes) but two different sets of class mechanics.

Does that solve our conundrum?
