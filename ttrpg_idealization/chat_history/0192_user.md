# Turn 192 — User

A1. Do Boons and Banes cancel one-for-one?

Yes. One boon removes one bane and vice versa.

A2. Can Boons stack beyond +3B?

We'll poach PF2e's types and stacking rules. That will also make it easier to conver 3.5e content since we can just map "D&D types A,B,C" to "Our System's Type Y"

A3. Can Banes stack beyond +3X?

No hard cap on banes. They stop stacking after the die steps-down. If we decide to have different types for the Boons, we could apply the same for the Bane. OBS: Regardless of the rest of the decisions, you can't stack boons or banes from the same source. Like casting the same spell 3 times to get 3 Boons or cause 3 Banes on the same creature that way.

A4. Does a Bane remove the highest die, a random die, or simply reduce pool volume?

Oooo. Ignoring the N highest rolls would hurt to watch. I think I like that. Would it be a lot more complicated than just rolling less dice?

A5. Does a Bane affect pool volume before or after Competency Rank?

As SotDL. After all the other calculations.

A6. Does a Bane suppress a Floor?

Yes. Just like a Boon bumps the floor, so does a Bane lowers it.

A7. Can a Die Step-Down and Bane coexist?

Yes. Boons and Banes are the very last thing to be computed.

A8. Do Boons improve multi-success counts?

Yes. Boons will work the same way as any other dice in the pool.

B1. What exactly counts as a tie?

Hmm. We can compare the other dice in the pool, in order to try and break the tie then. And if by some divine providence both pools have the same amount of dice and they have the same die face results, then defender wins.

B2. Does the defender win ties in noncombat contests?

Yes. The one that initiates is the "attacker", the other party is the "defender". Not that everything needs a tie breaker. A race can actually end on a tie.

B3. How are opposed multi-success results calculated?

Highest faces. Then the tiebreaker rules come into effect if necessary.

B4. Can the defender generate a superior defensive result?

As fair as the basic framework goes, just success and fail. Class features and feats might enable other things.

B5. How do Floors interact with ties?

The only thing the floor does is cap the lower band of the result, it won't change any of the other mechanics directly. Equal results will use the previously established tie rules.

B6. Do conditions that prevent reactions affect ties?

The conditions will spell out their own rules.

C1. How should Legendary capability exceed ordinary competency without larger dice?

More dice up to a point, then automatic successes after that. The ida is to pair that with DCs that need more than one success on the Difficulty side of the equation, so where's there might be a DC 9(3) a player might have a pool of 5d12 for that check with one guaranteed success on top of that. The thing about the guaranteed success is that it automatically passes checks that need 1 success regardless of the actual DC number, so a pool of 4d8 with one automatic success on top could pass a regular DC 9.

C2. Should high-tier effects increase pool volume beyond 5dX?

Sure. We won't impose a "arbitrary" hard cap, but we will control the pool by way of the content (Ancestries, Classes, Feats, Spells, Skill investment, etc) that provide more dice to the pool, staying mostly around that 5dX natural (unbuffed, unequipped, etc) limit.

C3. How should Mythic characters interact with DC 12?

See C1. It's basically a continuation of that with more dice, more guaranteed success and more difficult DCs. We can go as high as DC 12 after all. The thing about Mythic will be less about the numeric progression, and more about the effects the characters will have access to such as manipulate the difficulty, choosing the result of one or more dice from their roll etc.

C4. Should Mythic effects be stronger mathematically or broader fictionally?

Both but leaning more to the second option, with mythic characters having access to effects (such as Spell Traditions) that non-mythic have very limited access to.

C5. How should Legendary characters overcome lower-tier immunities?

As far as the basic rules framework goes, no. That's the purview of specific effects from Classes, Feats, etc.

C6. How should high-tier equipment exceed ordinary equipment?

From a game design philosophy, everything you mentioned + things like dealing (full or a portion of) fixed damage. Ignoring certain kinds of attacks or defenses, etc. The specifics are for the specific items and crafting rules to describe. A godly equipment might give 1 permanent boon a fixed success and an immunity of some sort, for example.

D1. Should every subsystem use the same ceiling?

Yes, in the veins of PF2e so that it's easier to reason over the math, make every check more intuitive, and enable cross system interactions (an ability where you parry a spell with a weapon?).

D2. Should every high-tier advantage be visible in probability?

No. Every tier will increase odds of success compared to the previous tier, but the higher the tier the more non-probabilistic advantages will factor into the game.

D3. Should exceptional effects be universal or tagged?

Both really, but primarily from specific content/effects. We discussed the specific of how the baseline progression framework will give automatic successes past a certain point (where there are no larger dice) for example.
