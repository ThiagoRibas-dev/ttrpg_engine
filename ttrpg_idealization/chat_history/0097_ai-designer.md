Alchemy as Craft and Knowledge

I agree that Alchemy should not be a Magic Mastery tradition.

Alchemy is fundamentally a method of producing effects through substances, preparation, and physical processes. It should therefore belong to the Craft and Knowledge architecture.

Knowledge — Alchemy and Pharmacology

Used to:

Identify compounds.
Understand poisons and medicines.
Analyze magical or pseudo-magical substances.
Design formulas.
Diagnose chemical effects.
Recognize contamination.
Understand dosage and interaction.
Craft — Alchemy

Used to:

Produce potions.
Distill toxins.
Brew acids.
Create explosives.
Prepare mutagens.
Manufacture antidotes.
Refine reagents.
Prepare pseudo-magical compounds.
Alchemical effects

An alchemical effect may reproduce the result of a spell without being a spell.

For example:

text
Alchemical Fire
Knowledge: Alchemy and Pharmacology
Craft: Alchemy
Traits: Fire, Area, Persistent
School Classification: None, unless a magical version exists

Or it could deliberately imitate a spell:

text
Potion of Invisibility
Craft: Alchemy
Knowledge: Alchemy and Pharmacology
Spell Analogue: Illusion
Traditions Imitated: Arcane or Shadow

This keeps Alchemy useful to:

Noncasters.
Artificers.
Rogues.
Rangers.
Scholars.
Craft-focused characters.
Spellcasters who want consumable or prepared effects.

It also gives us a clean distinction:

Magic changes reality through a tradition.
Alchemy changes reality through material preparation.

Psychic/Psionic Power

Your hesitation is justified. Psychic power has a different identity from most traditions.

If Psychic becomes a Magic Mastery tradition

It would function like:

text
Magic Mastery: Psychic
Spell Slots: Vancian
Focus: Used for mental exertion and metapsychic effects
Schools: Enchantment, Divination, Illusion, Evocation, Transmutation

Advantages:

Simple integration with the existing magic system.
Psychic spells can use the same spellbook and preparation structure.
Easy to convert D&D and PF content.
Fits the “every supernatural ability uses a tradition” model.

Disadvantages:

Psychic characters may feel like another type of wizard.
Vancian preparation is thematically awkward for spontaneous thought.
Telepathy and telekinesis may feel artificially divided into spell entries.
Psionic powers may lose their resource and augmentation identity.
If Psychic becomes a separate non-Vancian system

It could use:

A distinct Psionic Power competency.
Focus as the primary resource.
Mental exertion and strain.
At-will disciplines.
Techniques instead of prepared spells.
Augmentation tiers.
Backlash from overexertion.
Power manifestations shaped by intent.

Example:

text
Psionic Discipline: Telekinesis
Base Effect: Move a Close or Near object.
Augment: Increase scale, range, precision, or duration.
Cost: Focus or Stamina.
Risk: Mental strain when pushed beyond normal limits.

Advantages:

Strongly differentiates psychic characters.
Avoids forcing thoughts into Vancian spell slots.
Makes Focus a meaningful in-world resource.
Allows a small number of flexible powers instead of a huge spell list.
Feels closer to dedicated psionic systems.

Disadvantages:

Adds a second supernatural engine.
Requires its own advancement, conversion, and balance work.
Risks making Psychic characters more flexible than Vancian casters.
Requires clear boundaries between psychic powers and Enchantment, Divination, Illusion, and Telekinesis-style spells.
My recommendation

Keep Psychic provisionally outside the Magic Mastery tradition list.

Treat it as a possible parallel system:

Psychic/Psionic Power: a non-Vancian supernatural discipline powered primarily by Focus.

This does not permanently decide the issue, but preserves the possibility of giving it a genuinely different identity.

A future character could then have:

text
Magic Mastery: Arcane
Psionic Discipline: Telepathy

or:

text
Magic Mastery: Dream
Psionic Discipline: Oneiromancy

The major rule we will need later is:

No character should gain both full Vancian magic and full Psionic flexibility without meaningful opportunity cost.

Traditions and Schools

The overlap you identified is one of the strongest aspects of this system.

A spell is not defined by one category. It has multiple descriptors:

text
Spell:
  School: Necromancy
  Tradition: Life, Divine, Primal
  Mantles: Life, Creation, Guardian
  Traits: Healing, Touch, Sustained

Another spell can use the same school but a different tradition:

text
Spell:
  School: Necromancy
  Tradition: Death, Spirit, Shadow
  Mantles: Death, Repose, Communication
  Traits: Summoning, Ritual

This means “Necromancy” no longer means only evil corpse magic. It is the technical school dealing with life force, death, souls, and biological transformation. The tradition determines how that magic is accessed and expressed.

Current Status

The current architecture can now be summarized as:

text
Schools:
  The eight D&D-compatible technical classifications.

Traditions:
  Modular magical access and specialization packages.

Mantles:
  Conceptual domains such as Life, Death, Fate, Conflict, Knowledge,
  Natural World, Creation, and the Planes.

Traits:
  Mechanical descriptors such as Fire, Healing, Ritual, Area,
  Summoning, Touch, Sustained, and Mind-Affecting.

Alchemy:
  Knowledge and Craft rather than a magical tradition.
