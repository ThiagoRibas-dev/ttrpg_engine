# Turn 81 — AI-Designer

Agreed. These changes produce a cleaner architecture: skills represent durable domains of competence, while investigation, disguise, forgery, escape, and similar tasks become procedures built from those skills.

Below is a revised version of the Best Overall Architecture.

Revised Skill Architecture
Design Rule

Every character has:

Core Skills — broad, trainable domains.
Specialties — narrower fields under Craft, Knowledge, and Lore.
Activities — objectives resolved through one or more skills.
Tools and Equipment — may enable, improve, or constrain an activity.
Class and Feat Abilities — may unlock special procedures or allow unusual combinations.

A character does not roll “Investigation” by default. The player describes how they investigate, and the GM selects the relevant skill or skills.

Criticism of the Arrangement

This architecture is strong, but I see four issues that should remain under review.

1. Knowledge — Scribing may be misnamed

“Scribing” sounds primarily like a craft or profession. Its proposed functions include:

Reading
Writing
Languages
Translation
Document analysis
Calligraphy
Copying texts

I would probably rename it:

Knowledge — Languages and Scripts

Then create Craft — Scribing for the physical production of manuscripts and documents.

This gives a clean distinction:

Languages and Scripts: understand written communication.
Craft — Scribing: produce convincing or beautiful documents.
2. Anatomy and Healing may overlap with Alchemy and Pharmacology

This is not necessarily bad, but the boundaries should be explicit.

Anatomy and Healing: living bodies, wounds, disease, diagnosis, surgery.
Alchemy and Pharmacology: substances, poisons, drugs, antidotes, compounds.
Craft — Alchemy: manufacturing those substances.

A physician may know pharmacology without being an alchemist, and an alchemist may know poisons without being a surgeon.

3. Arcana and Spellcrafting may be too broad

This category currently combines:

Magical theory
Arcane history
Spell construction
Magical item analysis
Ritual practice
Enchantments

That may be acceptable, but it should not automatically encompass every magic tradition. Once the magic system is written, it may be better to divide it into:

Knowledge — Arcana
Knowledge — Spellcrafting
Knowledge — Magical Traditions

For now, keeping it combined is reasonable.

4. Lore and Knowledge must not become interchangeable

The system needs a clear adjudication rule:

Knowledge is formal, technical, systematic, or academic. Lore is focused familiarity with a particular subject, place, group, culture, creature, or tradition.

If a player asks, “Do I know how this works?” use Knowledge.

If they ask, “Do I know this thing, place, people, or tradition?” use Lore.
