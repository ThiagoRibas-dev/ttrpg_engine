# Turn 376 — User

1. Define the difficulty categories

Given that we have a level by level Easy, Mediocre, Hard DC construction, do we really need those?

We could define these labels in relation to the 3 levels of DC, although I'd tweak the labels to describe something like Trivial, Routine, Moderate, Challenging, Extreme or the like, if so.

Or better yet, we can just say that it's a Easy Veteran Check, Hard Trained Check, etc. At the table, the Game Master will just use the numbers anyhow, this is more of a design language artifact, I think.

2. Define reference actor profiles

Don't we already have that information in the research notes, even if provisionally in the case of equipped? Not labeled as such, but still. Guess we can just formalize that information.

3. Define success type

For DCs, we have that defined in the research artifacts by level band/tier (or at least one suggestion per difficulty per tier). The distinction between one or two vectors is gating who can perform such check by pool size, for the most part.

The probability profile of Opposed successes kind of arise naturally from the common framework that everybody will follow. If you are in the Veteran Rank of Competency attacking with 4d8 and your opponent is in the same Rank with the bonuses to reach the same 4d8 for the defense, the defender will tend to win since the attacker loses ties. That's intended. One should attack weaker defenses of equally or more powerful foes.

4. Define probability bands

We can solidify bands those into the design canon.
They apply constantly across tiers. An easy check Veteran Tier Check by a decently prepared Veteran Tier character will have the same 80-85% chance as it would for a Trained Tier Check being performed by a decently prepared Trained Tier character, just like we plotted in the research documents.

When you go up tiers, get bigger dice and larger dice pools, checks from the previous Tier become much easier, but equal Tier checks will have constant chances by design. Kind of like Pathfinder 2e's math.

5. Define whether targets apply before or after equipment

We decided the bands and calculated the DCs after equipment or equivalent abilities. We assume a character with good (not necessarily best) equipment or temporary boosts. Basically, better than baseline but not as powerful as possible.

8. Define multiple-success expectations

And should probably be the first thing we do, since we might recalibrate the DCs a bit for each lvl x difficulty combination and provide more than one possibility with similar odds but that gate access to that check in different ways (bigger dice vs more dice), and that's going to inform the other decisions. We can say that Easy DCs are always 1 dice, Medium 1 or 2, Hard 2 or 3, as a convention, for example, then tweak the exact values.
