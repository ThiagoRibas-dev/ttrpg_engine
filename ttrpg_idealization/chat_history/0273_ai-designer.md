# Turn 273 — AI-Designer

That is a much better type vocabulary. It is broad enough to work across equipment, spells, circumstances, allies, environmental effects, and converted content without making the types synonymous with character-building sources.

The four types should describe the nature of the temporary influence, not who granted it.

Boon/Bane Types
1. Circumstance

A Circumstance effect comes from the immediate situation, environment, positioning, timing, visibility, terrain, or physical conditions surrounding the check.

It answers:

What is happening around the actor right now?

Examples
Flanking an opponent.
High ground.
Partial cover.
Darkness.
Strong wind.
Stable footing.
Slippery ground.
A concealed position.
Fighting underwater.
Attacking from surprise.
A well-prepared ambush.
Being restrained by terrain.
Fighting in cramped quarters.
A loud distraction.
A magical zone affecting the battlefield.
Circumstance Boons
text
Flanking:
  +1B Circumstance on the attack.

High ground:
  +1B Circumstance on ranged attacks.

Stable shooting platform:
  +1B Circumstance on Archery.

Target exposed in the open:
  +1B Circumstance on attacks against it.
Circumstance Banes
text
Partial cover:
  +1X Circumstance on the attack.

Heavy rain:
  +1X Circumstance on Archery.

Unstable footing:
  +1X Circumstance on Athletics or Acrobatics.

Darkness:
  +1X Circumstance on sight-dependent checks.

Circumstance is usually:

Local.
Temporary.
Position-dependent.
Capable of changing from moment to moment.

A Circumstance effect should not represent permanent training, a character’s general morale, or the inherent quality of equipment.

2. Enhancement

An Enhancement effect directly improves the performance, quality, force, precision, resilience, or output of the actor, object, weapon, armor, or spell.

It answers:

What is making this thing perform better than its normal baseline?

Examples
A magical weapon.
A spell that strengthens an ally.
A potion.
A temporary physical transformation.
A superior tool.
A magical ammunition effect.
A weapon with a temporary energy charge.
An armor-hardening effect.
A temporary increase in spell potency.
A specialized magical implement.
Enhancement Boons
text
Magic weapon:
  +1B Enhancement on attacks with that weapon.

Blessed ammunition:
  +1B Enhancement against undead.

Potion of Giant Strength:
  +1B Enhancement on qualifying Strength checks.

Spell amplification:
  +1B Enhancement on the next spell attack.
Enhancement Banes
text
Damaged weapon:
  +1X Enhancement on attacks with that weapon.

Rust-covered armor:
  +1X Enhancement on relevant protection checks.

Cracked magical focus:
  +1X Enhancement on casting checks through that focus.

Enhancement should generally describe an improvement or degradation of capability, not a change in the surrounding circumstances.

A flaming sword has an Enhancement effect if the magic improves its attack. The fact that the target is standing in oil is a Circumstance effect.

Enhancement may come from:

Equipment.
Spells.
Potions.
Temporary transformations.
Alchemical effects.
Magical zones.
Supernatural conditions.

It is not an “Equipment Bonus” category. Equipment is merely one possible source.

3. Morale

A Morale effect represents emotional force, confidence, fear, inspiration, social pressure, intimidation, hope, despair, group cohesion, or psychological momentum.

It answers:

How is the actor’s emotional and social state affecting performance?

Morale Boons
text
An ally’s Leadership rally:
  +1B Morale on the next Willpower or attack check.

Fighting beside a trusted commander:
  +1B Morale against fear.

A victorious battle:
  +1B Morale for the next engagement.

A sacred oath:
  +1B Morale while pursuing the sworn objective.
Morale Banes
text
Frightened:
  +1X Morale on attacks against the source of fear.

Witnessing an ally fall:
  +1X Morale on the next check.

Being intimidated:
  +1X Morale on resistance to the intimidator.

A broken formation:
  +1X Morale on coordinated actions.

Morale should be distinct from:

Willpower itself.
Deception or Intimidation as Skills.
A permanent Class feature.
A generic magical enhancement.

A character can be highly trained in Willpower but temporarily suffer a Morale Bane from fear. Conversely, a character with modest Willpower can receive a Morale Boon from a leader.

Morale can affect:

Attacks.
Defense.
Skill checks.
Willpower.
Initiative.
Group coordination.
Retreat and surrender behavior.

Morale is especially useful for:

Leadership.
Underlings.
Rabble.
Mass combat.
Fear effects.
Social conflicts.
Religious or ideological groups.
4. Competence

A Competence effect represents temporary assistance from training, instruction, advice, specialized knowledge, coordinated technique, professional guidance, or a focused performance state.

It answers:

What makes the actor temporarily better at performing this type of task?

Competence Boons
text
An expert demonstrates a technique:
  +1B Competence on the learner’s next check.

A detailed plan:
  +1B Competence on a related Activity.

Using a specialized manual:
  +1B Competence on a technical Lore or Craft check.

An assistant stabilizes a surgical procedure:
  +1B Competence on the primary Medicine check.

A scout provides targeting information:
  +1B Competence on an attack against a marked target.
Competence Banes
text
Working outside one’s training:
  +1X Competence on a specialized task.

Confusing or contradictory instructions:
  +1X Competence on the task.

Using an unfamiliar technique under pressure:
  +1X Competence on the first attempt.

Loss of concentration on a complex procedure:
  +1X Competence on the next stage.

Competence should not replace permanent Skill Competency Rank.

The distinction is:

text
Competency Rank:
  Your actual invested, durable mastery.

Competence Boon:
  Temporary help, preparation, instruction, or focus
  applied to a specific task or short sequence.

A Trained character receiving a Competence Boon does not become Expert. They simply perform one task with temporary assistance.

Competence may come from:

An assistant.
Preparation.
A tactical plan.
A tool manual.
A mentor.
A scouting report.
A temporary focus state.
Team coordination.
A familiar procedure.
A class or feat effect that specifically provides temporary assistance.

It should not represent the permanent result of spending Skill allocations.

Summary Table
Type	Core Question	Typical Sources	Typical Duration
Circumstance	What is happening around the actor?	Terrain, cover, positioning, weather, visibility	Momentary or scene
Enhancement	What directly improves or degrades performance?	Equipment, spells, potions, transformations	Short-term or item-bound
Morale	How does emotional/social force affect performance?	Leadership, fear, victory, despair, oaths	Momentary, scene, or encounter
Competence	What provides temporary task-specific expertise?	Assistance, preparation, instruction, plans, tools	Check, Activity, or short sequence
Untyped Effects

A rare effect may have no type.

An untyped Boon or Bane:

Can stack with typed effects.
May stack with other untyped effects unless the source says otherwise.
Must be used sparingly.
Should be reserved for unusually fundamental or exceptional effects.

Examples might include:

A unique artifact.
A mythic permission.
A highly specific Class feature.
A once-per-scene supernatural effect.
A rule explicitly intended to stack with everything.

Untyped should not mean “stronger by default.” It means:

This effect does not belong to the ordinary typed stacking categories.

Same-Source Rule

Regardless of type:

The same named source cannot apply the same Boon or Bane more than once to the same target or check unless it explicitly says otherwise.

Examples:

text
Three castings of the same spell:
  Do not provide three copies of the same Boon.

Three allies using the same aura:
  Do not automatically provide three copies of the same Morale Boon.

Two different spells with different named effects:
  May stack if their types and specific rules permit it.
Recommended Baseline Rule

The baseline framework could state:

Boons and Banes are classified as Circumstance, Enhancement, Morale, Competence, or Untyped. Effects of the same type do not stack; use only the strongest applicable effect of that type. Effects of different types stack normally. The same named source cannot apply the same Boon or Bane more than once to the same target or check. An untyped effect may stack with typed effects and other untyped effects unless its source says otherwise.

The main remaining question is whether “strongest applicable effect” should mean:

Keep the highest numerical Boon/Bane count.
Keep the effect with the greatest defined severity.
Let the specific effect state its priority.

My recommendation is the first for the basic system:

text
+1B Circumstance and +2B Circumstance
→ keep +2B Circumstance

Then special content can explicitly override that rule through a Permission.
