Vincent Baker — RPG Design and Development

Vincent Baker’s design essays on creating Powered by the Apocalypse games are useful as process and theory references, not as mechanical templates for this project.

Relevant themes include:

Starting from intended play experience.
Identifying the underlying model of action and consequence.
Designing procedures that express the game’s priorities.
Drafting a new RPG from an existing game without copying its assumptions.
Distinguishing specific mechanics from the design model beneath them.

Useful reference: Powered by the Apocalypse, Part 1

D&D Next Playtest Lessons

The D&D Next playtest is a useful case study in large-scale iterative design. Relevant lessons include:

Test mathematical balance and thematic coherence separately.
Examine player expectations as well as intended rules behavior.
Stress-test a system after stripping away its presentation and content trappings.
Use repeated play and feedback to identify contradictions and friction.
Treat playtest evidence as evidence for design decisions, not as automatic authority.

Useful discussion: The Role of Playtesting in Game Design

General Game-Design References

These are not TTRPG-specific, but may be useful for process and theory:

Game Design Workshop — Tracy Fullerton: iterative design, prototyping, observation, and playtesting.
Rules of Play — Katie Salen and Eric Zimmerman: formal systems, rules, emergence, and meaningful choice.
Challenges for Game Designers — Brenda Brathwaite and Ian Schreiber: focused design exercises and subsystem prototyping.

The resources reinforce the value of separating:

They also support the current project workflow:

Establish design intent.
Define the mathematical model.
Build a minimal prototype.
Stress-test the prototype.
Observe actual player and GM behavior.
Separate mechanical failure from presentation failure.
Revise and consolidate only after review.
Application to the Current Project

The most relevant process principles are:

Keep the foundational framework separate from Classes, Feats, Spells, Ancestries, and other content.
Calculate probabilities before finalizing target bands.
Use representative profiles rather than isolated examples.
Treat Equipment as an independent progression vector.
Test multiple Difficulty Vectors with similar probabilities but different capability gates.
Preserve rejected designs as historical research.
Do not let research artifacts become accidental rules.
Avoid solving content problems by adding framework exceptions.
Use playtesting only after the base framework and MVP content library are sufficiently stable.
