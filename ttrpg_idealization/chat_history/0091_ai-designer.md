The eight schools classify the technical function of the spell. The traditions determine who can access, learn, prepare, and specialize in it.

A spell may belong to several traditions without becoming a different spell.

1. Arcane and Divine Naming

I would initially keep Arcane and Divine as the familiar player-facing names, while giving them more precise formal definitions.

Arcane

Magic accessed through:

Study
Formula
Intuition
Symbolic understanding
Personal will
Manipulation of raw magical forces
Research and experimentation

Possible formal alternatives:

Formulaic
Thaumaturgic
Scholastic
Aethereal
High Magic
Willworking

My preference is:

Arcane as the common name; Thaumaturgic as an optional formal or setting-specific term.

Divine

Magic accessed through:

A god
A cosmic power
A sacred covenant
Devotion
Prayer
Religious authority
A higher-order intelligence
A metaphysical source of life, judgment, or purpose

Possible alternatives:

Theurgic
Channeled
Consecrated
Revelatory
Sacred
Covenantal

My preference is:

Divine as the common name; Theurgic as the technical classification.

“Channeled” is understandable, but it describes the process rather than the source. “Divine/Theurgic” makes the distinction clearer:

text
Arcane / Thaumaturgic: the caster manipulates magic.
Divine / Theurgic: the caster channels power through a relationship or authority.

Nature does not necessarily need to be Divine. A druid could channel nature directly through the Primal tradition, while a nature god’s priest uses Divine magic.

8. Main Criticism

The biggest risk is that traditions such as Blood, Death, Shadow, Spirit, and Divine may overlap heavily. That is not automatically a problem. It becomes a problem only if they have identical:

Spell lists.
Casting procedures.
Resource costs.
Narrative consequences.
Tactical strengths.

The distinction should be:

Tradition	Primary Relationship
Divine	Authority, covenant, worship, sacred source
Primal	Living systems and natural forces
Spirit	Negotiation with souls and spirits
Shadow	Absence, secrecy, fear, concealment
Blood	Vital essence, sacrifice, lineage
Death	Mortality, decay, undeath
Dream	Consciousness, memory, subjective reality

This allows the same technical school—especially Necromancy or Enchantment—to appear in multiple traditions while retaining a different identity.

My strongest recommendation is to treat the twelve initial traditions as access and specialization packages, not rigid isolated spell lists. A spell can belong to two, three, or even four traditions, while class/path features determine which traditions a character can access and how they specialize within them.
