Level-by-Level Progression: Deep Comparative Analysis vs. Shadow of the Demon Lord

To understand exactly how our 20-Level / 6-Tier Universal Progression Engine expands upon and elevates Shadow of the Demon Lord (SotDL / Weird Wizard), we must dissect the level-by-level anatomy of both games.

While SotDL packs its character progression into 10 rapid levels (divided across 3 modular paths: Novice, Expert, Master), our system expands that exact modular path concept across 20 Character Levels and 6 High Fantasy Tiers (Trained -> Veteran -> Master -> Hero -> Legend -> Mythic [21+]).

This document provides a side-by-side anatomical comparison of every single advancement step from Level 1 to 20+, demonstrating why our mathless step-pool engine delivers superior simulationist depth, tactical choice, and universal actor consistency (DEC-011).

1.1 Why 20 Levels Across 4-Level Bands is Superior to 10 Levels

In SotDL, leveling happens every 1-2 sessions (10 sessions total to finish a full campaign from 0 to 10). While fast, it forces compressed mechanical jumps (say, jumping from +1 Boonto+3 Boons in two sessions).

By structuring our game into 4-level bands per tier across 20 levels, we achieve D&D 3.5e/PF2e campaign longevity (months or years of play across 20 levels), while maintaining SotDL's zero dead levels design: every single level between path selections awards concrete Die Step-Ups (X+1), Domain Competency Ranks (Floor & +B), or Action Feats ([1A], [2A], [SET] triggers)!
