# Turn 77 — AI-Designer

Yes—this is a much stronger direction. The important distinction is:

A skill represents a transferable field of competence. An activity is a concrete objective resolved by selecting the relevant skill, approach, tools, and sometimes a class or feat feature.

That keeps the skill list meaningful without turning every recognizable action into its own skill.

1. Skill, Activity, Procedure, and Maneuver
Skill

A skill is a learned domain that can be applied to many situations.

Examples:

Lockpicking is probably too narrow as a skill.
Mechanics or Thievery is broad enough to cover locks, traps, delicate mechanisms, and improvised devices.
Disguise & Impersonation is probably too procedure-specific.
Deception, Performance, and perhaps Crafting can support it depending on the method.
Sailing is broad enough to be a skill.
Rigging a sail is an activity using Sailing, possibly with Crafting or Athletics support.
Activity

An activity is an objective that may involve one or more skills.

Examples:

Activity	Primary Skill	Possible Support
Climb a wall	Athletics	Crafting for equipment
Pick a lock	Thievery/Mechanics	Lore for unusual mechanisms
Impersonate a noble	Deception	Performance, Insight, Forgery tools
Decode a rune	Lore: Runes	Languages, Arcana tradition later
Navigate a ship	Sailing	Cartography, Survival
Interrogate a prisoner	Intimidation or Insight	Medicine, Deception
Train a beast	Beast Handling	Survival, Insight
Escape bonds	Athletics or Thievery	Crafting, specific tools
Fortify a campsite	Survival	Crafting, Engineering/Lore
Negotiate a treaty	Diplomacy	Law, History, Insight
Procedure

A procedure defines how the activity works mechanically.

For example:

Pick a Lock: Roll Thievery against the lock’s static DC. If the lock is unfamiliar, trapped, magical, or under pressure, the GM may require a supporting skill, tool, or special feature.

Maneuver

A maneuver is an intentional tactical action, usually resolved through an opposed roll.

Examples:

Trip
Disarm
Shove
Grapple
Overextend
Bleed
Feint
Called Shot

These should not generally be skills. They are actions performed using an appropriate skill or combat pool.

2. Proposed Skill Architecture

I would keep the five umbrellas, but refine their names slightly.

Domain 1: Combat Mastery

Physical and technical weapon proficiency.

Heavy Blades
Light Blades
Polearms and Spears
Axes and Picks
Hammers, Maces, and Flails
Archery
Exotic Weapons
Shield Defense
Martial Arts
Notes
Finesse and Throwable should absolutely be weapon traits, not skills.
Crossbows can remain part of Archery, with Mechanical or Loading traits determining their handling.
Grappling and Wrestling should not be a separate skill. They are combat applications of Martial Arts, usually opposed against the target’s Martial Arts, Athletics, or relevant defense.
A fighter may use Heavy Blades for a sword attack, but Trip, Disarm, or Overextend remains a maneuver layered onto that attack.
Exotic Weapons should be a player-named category, but the category must be approved and internally coherent: “chain weapons,” “firearms,” “double weapons,” “whips,” and so on.

One possible concern is that nine weapon skills are already highly granular. That is acceptable if weapon specialization is meant to be a major build axis. A character should not need every weapon skill to be combat-capable, but should become distinctly better within a chosen family.

Naval combat uses Sailing plus an appropriate weapon or command skill.
Beast Handling

Beast Taming and Training should be activities within a broader skill:

Activities include:

Calm a frightened beast

Train a mount
Break an animal’s aggression
Identify animal behavior
Coordinate a trained animal in combat

This can use Insight for emotional reading, Survival for wild animals, and Riding for mounted control.

Domain 3: Subterfuge and Perception

Stealth, sensory awareness, tactical movement, and covert manipulation.

Disguise or perhaps no separate Disguise skill
Moving Silently and Hide

These should remain separate.

Moving Silently defeats auditory detection.
Hide defeats visual detection.
A character attempting to sneak past a guard may need both, but normally only the relevant one is rolled at a time.
Listen and Spot

These should also remain separate if you want simulationist perception.

Listen detects sound, voices, movement, and hidden activity.
Spot detects visual details, concealed objects, ambushes, and movement.

“Notice something” is not a universal Perception skill. The GM identifies the relevant sense and calls for the appropriate skill.

Thievery

I strongly recommend adding Thievery as a broad skill.

It covers:

Pickpocketing
Lockpicking
Trap disarming
Concealing stolen objects
Bypassing simple security
Tampering with mechanisms
Escape artistry using tools or clever handwork

This is better than making Lockpicking and Trap Disarming independent skills.

Shadowing

I would not make Shadowing a separate skill. It is an activity using:

Moving Silently
Hide
Spot
Survival
Deception, when maintaining a cover identity

The player’s chosen method determines the skill.

Disguise and Impersonation

These should also be activities rather than a standalone skill.

Possible approaches:

Deception: maintain the false identity under questioning.
Performance: convincingly portray mannerisms, speech, and behavior.
Crafting: create the disguise, costume, or prosthetics.
Insight: study the person being impersonated.
Forgery: create supporting documents.

This produces a more interesting result than a single “Disguise” roll.

4. Craft — X
Crafting as the general skill

Crafting is the ability to make, repair, modify, and evaluate physical objects.

It covers general competence with tools and materials, but should not make a character equally good at every trade.

A character chooses one or more Craft specialties.

Suggested Craft specialties
Armorsmithing
Weaponsmithing

Tailoring and Weaving

Stoneworking

Cooking and Brewing
Herbalism
Clockwork and Mechanisms
Trapmaking
Forgery and Document Production
Disguise Construction
Wand, Rune, or Magical Item Crafting after the magic system is defined
Recommended rule

A character does not need a separate skill rank in every craft. Instead:

Crafting is the parent skill; each specialty is a trained field that determines what the character can make or repair.

For example:

Crafting — Blacksmithing
Crafting — Shipwright
Crafting — Alchemy

A character may be a Master Blacksmith without being competent at alchemy.

Why not make every craft a full skill?

Because that creates dozens of isolated skills that rarely matter in the same campaign. Specialties preserve granularity without requiring every craft to occupy the same character-sheet space as Archery or Diplomacy.

8. Cartography and Mapmaking

I recommend making Cartography a specialty of either Knowledge or Crafting, depending on the intended use.

Reading a map: Knowledge — Geography
Creating a reliable map: Cartography
Surveying terrain: Cartography + Survival
Navigating by map: Cartography or Sailing
Forging a map: Cartography + Forgery
Identifying a map’s creator or era: Lore

My preferred structure is:

Cartography as a Knowledge/Crafting specialty, not a universal standalone skill.

It is a real field of competence, but it does not need to compete for the same space as Athletics or Diplomacy.

10. Recommended Activity Resolution Model

To prevent activities from becoming multi-roll bureaucracy, use this structure:

Standard activity

One skill roll.

Pick a lock: Thievery.

Complex activity

One primary roll, with a supporting skill changing the fictional conditions, available approaches, or consequences—not automatically adding a numerical bonus.

Impersonate a duke:

Deception is primary.
Performance establishes mannerisms.
Crafting creates the disguise.
Lore provides knowledge of court customs.
Forgery creates documents.

The character does not necessarily roll all four. The player chooses an approach, or the GM calls for stages when the activity genuinely contains separate obstacles.

Team activity

One lead character rolls; assistants contribute through preparation, tools, positioning, or a suitable assistance rule.

Class or feat interaction

Class abilities should alter what is possible:

A Rogue can attempt complex locks without specialized tools.
A Ranger can navigate wilderness without a map.
A Fighter can use Martial Arts to grapple as part of an attack.
A Courtier can use Diplomacy to establish formal negotiation rights.
A Wizard may identify magical runes through an Arcane tradition skill.

This keeps class identity meaningful without turning class features into skill replacements.

This produces approximately 35–40 meaningful parent skills, while allowing a much larger number of specialties beneath Craft, Lore, and Knowledge.

That seems like the best middle ground between:

The original 65-skill pulverized model.
A very compressed 28-skill model.
A chaotic list where every activity becomes its own skill.

The main structural question for us is whether Craft, Lore, and Knowledge should be parent skills with specialties, or whether each specialty should be an independently ranked skill. My recommendation is parent skill plus specialty, with only the specialty fields relevant to a character appearing on the character sheet.
