Notes on weird traditions.md

These notes are extremely useful because they reveal that Weird Wizard uses the word tradition for several different design functions at once.

Some of its traditions are genuinely different magical practices. Others are really:

D&D-style schools.
Elemental domains.
Casting sources.
Tactical roles.
Character themes.
Special effect packages.

That distinction is important for our system because we have already decided to preserve the eight D&D schools as technical classifications.

12. My Main Conclusions from the Notes

The Weird Wizard material suggests several useful design principles for us:

Elemental traditions should usually be specialties under Elemental, not five entirely separate core traditions.
D&D schools should remain separate from traditions, because many Weird Wizard traditions are functionally schools.
Rune, Alchemy, War, Spirit, Shadow, and Dream are excellent true traditions because they imply distinct procedures and character identities.
Chaos and Order are better cosmic mantles or advanced traditions than ordinary starting traditions.
Time and Space should be restricted or advanced, especially because they interfere with action economy and positioning.
Technomancy should be setting-dependent, sitting between Arcane, Rune, Alchemy, and Craft.
Celestial should be a Divine specialization, unless the game specifically defines all higher-power magic as celestial.
Natural should be a Primal specialization or alternate label, while Primal remains the more mythic formal tradition.
SotDL-style identity comes from combining traditions, not from making every theme a separate school.
Each tradition needs a mechanical procedure or relationship with magic, not merely a color palette.

The most promising initial tradition framework is therefore:

text
Foundational:
  Arcane
  Divine / Theurgic
  Primal / Natural
  Spirit
  Shadow

Specialist:
  Elemental
  Rune
  Alchemy
  War
  Dream
  Psychic
  Blood
  Death
  Eldritch / Void

Advanced:
  Time
  Space
  Chaos
  Order
  Creation

This is broad enough to absorb Weird Wizard’s traditions, compatible with the Ardent mantles, and still leaves the eight D&D schools available as the technical classification layer.
