# Turn 83 — AI-Designer

Agreed on all three points.

1. Languages and Scripts / Craft — Scribing

This distinction should be formalized.

Knowledge — Languages and Scripts

Covers:

Reading and writing
Identifying scripts and alphabets
Translation
Linguistic structure
Ancient writing systems
Ciphers and coded language
Recognizing authorship or scribal traditions
Understanding written legal, religious, or magical forms
Craft — Scribing

Covers:

Producing manuscripts
Copying scrolls
Calligraphy
Preparing seals
Creating official documents
Reproducing a document’s material appearance
Preparing illuminated texts
Creating physically convincing papers, inks, bindings, and seals

A forgery may require both:

Knowledge — Languages and Scripts to understand what the document says and how it should be written.
Craft — Scribing to reproduce the physical document.
Deception to pass it off.
Lore — Law, Knowledge — Nobility, or another relevant field to make the content credible.

This creates a much better model than a single Forgery skill.

2. Knowledge — Nobility, Courts, and Etiquette

Yes, this should be added as a formal Knowledge skill.

I would name it:

Knowledge — Nobility, Courts, and Etiquette

It covers:

Heraldry
Noble houses
Titles and precedence
Court ceremonies
Formal introductions
Protocol
Dress codes
Gift customs
Banquets and formal occasions
Courtly insults and indirect language
Diplomatic etiquette
Rules of audience
Social consequences of breaches of protocol

This differs from the Social skills:

Task	Skill
Know who outranks whom	Knowledge — Nobility, Courts, and Etiquette
Follow proper protocol	Knowledge — Nobility, Courts, and Etiquette
Persuade a noble	Diplomacy
Detect a noble’s hidden motive	Insight
Impersonate a noble	Deception, Performance, and relevant Knowledge
Command a noble retinue	Leadership
Know a specific house’s secret history	Lore — Noble House X

This skill can also be relevant outside aristocratic settings. It may apply to:

Bureaucratic institutions
Military hierarchy
Religious hierarchy
Guild formalities
Diplomatic protocol

If the campaign needs more separation later, those can become Lore specialties rather than new universal skills.

3. Naming the Magic Knowledge Skill

I would not use Knowledge — Arcana and Spellcrafting as the permanent name.

The problem is that “Arcana” can imply a specific magical tradition, while the skill is meant to represent general magical knowledge before traditions are finalized.

Option 1: Knowledge — Magic and Spellcrafting

This is the clearest broad name.

It covers:

Magical theory
Spell structure
Magical symbols
Enchantments
Ritual construction
Identifying magical effects
Understanding spell interactions
Designing or modifying spells
Analyzing magical phenomena

This is my preferred name if the skill is intended to cover both theory and the practical construction of spells.

Option 2: Knowledge — Magical Theory

This is the cleanest academic name.

It covers:

Principles of magic
Magical phenomena
Spell structures
Magical laws
Arcane history
Interactions between magical effects

However, it does not clearly communicate practical spell design.

Option 3: Knowledge — Spellcrafting

This is the most mechanically direct name.

It covers:

Analyzing spells
Creating spells
Modifying spells
Identifying magical effects
Understanding magical components

The weakness is that it sounds more like a practical casting-related skill than a general knowledge domain.

Recommendation

Use:

Knowledge — Magic and Spellcrafting

This is broad enough to cover magical theory and applied magical construction without committing us to “Arcana” as a universal tradition.

Later, we can decide whether “Spellcrafting” should be a formal subfield or a procedure within the skill.

4. Separate Magic Knowledge from Magic Proficiency

I agree that magical knowledge and the ability to cast should remain separate.

A character may be:

A scholar who understands spells but cannot cast them.
A natural caster who can perform magic without understanding its theoretical structure.
A priest who performs divine rites but knows little about other magic.
A wizard with deep theoretical knowledge and specialized Arcane casting.
A noncaster with Lore — Necromancy or Knowledge — Magic and Spellcrafting.

This suggests two separate layers.

Knowledge and Technical Practice

This contains:

Knowledge — Magic and Spellcrafting

It answers:

What is this magical effect?
How does this spell work?
What are its components?
What is the likely danger?
How could this magical structure be modified?
What tradition or source produced it?
Magic Mastery

This is a new domain containing the actual magical proficiency skills.

Possible future skills include:

Arcana
Divine
Primal
Shadow
Time
Occult
Elemental
Other traditions established by the spellcasting system

These answer:

Can the character cast this spell?
How reliably can they invoke this tradition?
What types of magic can they prepare?
What magical actions are available?
How advanced are their spells?
Which class, path, or tradition abilities do they possess?

The precise names should wait until the magic subsystem is designed, but the separate domain should be established now.

6. What a Domain Can Do Mechanically

A domain should not merely be a label above a skill. It should be a higher-level design object.

Domain-level class features

A class may grant:

Automatic advancement in a domain.
Access to a domain’s advanced skills.
A special action within a domain.
A domain-specific reaction.
Access to domain feats.
Permission to combine skills in unusual ways.

Examples:

A Fighter gains Combat Mastery features.
A Rogue gains Subterfuge and Perception features.
A Wizard gains Magic Mastery and Knowledge — Magic and Spellcrafting features.
An Artificer gains Craft and Production features.
A Courtier gains Social and Influence plus Knowledge — Nobility, Courts, and Etiquette.
Domain-level feats

Examples:

Combat Mastery Feat: Use a maneuver after a specific weapon attack.
Athletics Domain Feat: Ignore certain terrain-based movement restrictions.
Subterfuge Domain Feat: Perform a Thievery activity during a larger action.
Knowledge Domain Feat: Recall information without prior preparation.
Craft Domain Feat: Repair equipment during a short breather.
Magic Mastery Feat: Cast a tradition-specific spell as a special reaction.
Domain-level equipment

Some equipment may interact with a domain rather than one skill:

A thieves’ kit interacts with Subterfuge and Perception.
A surgeon’s kit interacts with Knowledge and Healing.
A navigator’s kit interacts with Survival, Sailing, and Cartography.
A spellbook interacts with Knowledge and Magic Mastery.
A forge interacts with Craft and Production.
Domain-level paths

Modular paths can be defined partly by domain:

Warrior: Combat Mastery
Scout: Survival and Subterfuge
Courtier: Social Influence and Nobility Knowledge
Artificer: Craft and Knowledge
Wizard: Magic Mastery and Magical Knowledge
Priest: Divine Mastery and Religions and Rites

This supports the desired class/path architecture without making domains replace individual skill ranks.

7. Important Caution: Do Not Add Domain Ranks Yet

I would not create a separate numerical Domain Rank in addition to skill Competency Ranks.

That risks creating two overlapping progressions:

Domain Rank
Individual Skill Rank

Instead, domains should initially function as:

Groupings for class features.
Prerequisite categories.
Feat and path tags.
Access structures.
Sources of domain-specific actions.
Thematic containers for equipment and procedures.

The actual check should still use the individual skill.

For example:

A Wizard may have the Magic Mastery domain and Arcana skill, but the Arcana skill is what determines the casting check.

A future rule may allow a domain feature to alter the pool, grant a special action, or bypass a restriction, but the domain itself should not automatically add a second proficiency value.

9. Final Criticism

The strongest aspect of this arrangement is that it now has clear separations:

Spot and Listen detect.
Insight interprets people.
Knowledge provides formal understanding.
Lore provides focused familiarity.
Craft produces or repairs.
Social skills influence.
Magic Mastery performs magic.
Activities combine these where appropriate.

The main remaining risk is skill-list growth. We now have:

9 Combat skills
7 Athletics/Survival skills
6 Subterfuge skills
9 Knowledge skills
6 Social skills
20+ Craft specialties
Open-ended Lore
Provisional Magic Mastery skills

That is acceptable for a high-option-density system, but the character sheet should not display every possible Craft specialty or Lore category. It should show only skills the character possesses, plus a controlled space for specialties.

The best sheet structure would therefore be:

text
DOMAIN
  Skill
  Competency Rank
  Specialty or Focus

For example:

text
KNOWLEDGE AND TECHNICAL PRACTICE
  Knowledge — Magic and Spellcrafting     Expert
  Knowledge — Nobility, Courts, Etiquette Trained
  Knowledge — Cartography                  Trained

CRAFT AND PRODUCTION
  Craft — Scribing                          Expert
  Craft — Alchemy                           Trained

LORE
  Lore — Imperial Law                       Expert
  Lore — Undead                             Trained

This preserves the domain architecture while preventing the character sheet from becoming a 100-entry encyclopedia.
