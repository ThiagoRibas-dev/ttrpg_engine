# Turn 79 — AI-Designer

The comparison strongly supports your revised direction, but it also reveals several holes. The major lesson is that the four editions use three different models:

D&D 3.5e / Pathfinder 1e: long lists of individually ranked skills, with many narrow applications folded into broader skills.
D&D 5e: very compressed universal skills, with tools and backgrounds carrying much of the specialization.
Pathfinder 2e: broad core skills plus an intentionally open-ended Lore system.

Your system should probably combine:

3.5e/PF1e’s specificity,
PF2e’s clean separation between broad skills and Lore,
and 5e’s use of tools and background competence.

Strengths

3.5e is very good at representing specialized training:

A character can be excellent at Disable Device but poor at social skills.
Knowledge domains are explicitly differentiated.
Craft and Profession are open-ended.
Languages and scholarly fields are represented separately.
Tumble, Escape Artist, Use Rope, and similar skills support detailed procedures.
Weaknesses
Many skills are too narrow or repetitive.
Listen, Search, and Spot create redundant perception bookkeeping.
Balance, Climb, Jump, Swim, and Tumble can feel like separate pieces of one physical competency.
Craft and Profession are underdefined despite having excellent structural potential.
Some skills exist mainly because the game requires a place to attach a specific rule.
Skill ranks, class skill costs, cross-class costs, and ability modifiers create substantial overhead.
Lesson for your system

3.5e demonstrates that granular skill identity is valuable, but its individual skill list should not be copied directly. We should preserve the domains while converting narrow procedures into activities.

Pathfinder 1e

PF1e largely retains the 3.5e architecture, with refinements such as:

Acrobatics combining Balance and Tumble.
Perception combining Listen, Search, and Spot.
Disable Device remaining a specialized skill.
Knowledge remaining divided into fields.
Linguistics handling languages and forgery.
Use Magic Device remaining separate.
Profession and Craft remaining open-ended.
Escape Artist, Handle Animal, Ride, and Sleight of Hand remaining distinct.
Strengths

PF1e shows how to consolidate without completely destroying specialization.

The best examples are:

Acrobatics combining Balance and Tumble.
Perception combining multiple sensory skills.
Linguistics combining language acquisition and written-language expertise.
Disable Device retaining a clear technical identity.
Weaknesses
Perception becomes an extremely dominant skill.
Acrobatics becomes overloaded.
Knowledge remains fragmented into many fields but is often difficult to adjudicate.
Craft, Profession, and Perform have a lot of conceptual overlap.
Skill investment is still heavily influenced by class-skill rules and rank economics.
Lesson for your system

PF1e is useful as a source for identifying which 3.5e skills can be combined. However, it still treats too many activities as skills.

Strengths

PF2e is the closest existing model to your intended structure.

It separates:

Broad transferable skills,
Specific actions performed with those skills,
Specialized Lore fields,
Feats that expand what a character can do.

For example:

Thievery covers many criminal and mechanical procedures.
Crafting covers a wide production domain.
Medicine covers multiple healing procedures.
Lore can represent Sailing Lore, Warfare Lore, Legal Lore, or a particular culture.
Skill feats distinguish ordinary competence from specialized techniques.

This is very close to your proposed idea that lockpicking, trap disarming, and forgery should be activities rather than independent skills.

Weaknesses
Some skills are still too broad for your desired simulationism.
Stealth combines hiding and moving silently, which your design may reasonably keep separate.
Perception is a universal derived capability rather than distinct Listen and Spot skills.
Nature, Arcana, Occultism, and Religion overlap somewhat as knowledge and tradition categories.
Lore can become either beautifully specific or wildly inconsistent depending on the campaign.
Lesson for your system

PF2e provides the best structural foundation:

Broad skills handle transferable competence. Skill actions handle procedures. Lore handles focused knowledge. Feats unlock exceptional applications.

Lesson for your system

5e demonstrates the usefulness of tools, but its handling of crafting and specialized knowledge is too vague for your goals. You should use tools as requirements and fictional permissions, not as substitutes for skills.

2. Holes in the Current List

Your current list is already structurally sound, but I see several important omissions.

Hole 1: Acrobatics, Balance, and Tumbling

You currently have:

Athletics
Jumping
Moving Silently
Martial Arts

But no explicit skill for:

Balance
Tumbling
Controlled falls
Acrobatics
Rolling through danger
Narrow ledges
Inverted movement
Evasion through terrain

You have two viable options.

Option A: Add Acrobatics

Add:

Acrobatics: Balance, tumbling, controlled falls, evasive movement, and precise body control.

This is the cleanest option and follows PF1e/PF2e.

Option B: Divide the functions
Athletics: forceful movement and endurance.
Acrobatics: balance, tumbling, precision movement.
Jumping: distance and vertical leaps.

My recommendation is to add Acrobatics and eventually fold Jumping into it or Athletics.

A clean division would be:

Activity	Skill
Long jump	Athletics
High jump	Athletics
Balance on a narrow surface	Acrobatics
Tumble through an enemy’s space	Acrobatics
Reduce falling impact	Acrobatics
Sprint or force march	Athletics
Climb	Athletics
Swim	Swimming

This avoids making Jumping a full skill.

Hole 2: Investigation and Search

You have:

Listen
Spot
Lore
Knowledge
Thievery

But no explicit skill for deliberate examination.

You need a skill or activity covering:

Searching a room
Finding hidden compartments
Examining clues
Reconstructing what happened
Identifying tampering
Comparing evidence
Analyzing physical details

I recommend adding:

Investigation: Deliberate examination, evidence analysis, reconstruction, and systematic searching.

This should be distinct from:

Spot: noticing something visually.
Listen: noticing something audibly.
Insight: interpreting motives or emotions.
Thievery: bypassing or manipulating a mechanism.
Lore/Knowledge: knowing what an object or clue means.

I recommend a broad skill or specialty:

Languages and Scripts: Reading, writing, translating, identifying scripts, and understanding linguistic structure.

It can be organized as a specialty system:

Languages and Scripts — Elven

Languages and Scripts — Draconic Runes

However, ordinary spoken languages may remain a character feature rather than a skill rank.

Hole 5: Nature and the Living World

Survival covers much of this, but your Lore and Knowledge system needs a home for:

Plants
Animals
Weather
Ecology
Natural hazards
Monsters
Magical biology

You can handle this in two ways.

Broad approach

Add:

Granular approach

Use:

Lore — Beasts for traditional monster knowledge.
Knowledge — Biology for formal natural study.
Beast Handling for animal interaction.

For your system, I recommend the latter, with Nature retained as a Lore/Knowledge specialty rather than necessarily a full universal skill.

Hole 6: Deception-Adjacent Disguise

You correctly identified that Disguise and Impersonation should not necessarily be independent skills. However, the system should explicitly define them as activities:

Disguise and Impersonation may use Deception, Performance, Crafting, Insight, Lore, and Forgery depending on the method.

This should be documented so that players do not assume it is simply “roll Deception.”

Hole 7: Forgery

Forgery is similar.

It should be an activity that may involve:

Craft — Forgery
Languages and Scripts
Deception
Lore — Heraldry
Knowledge — Law
Thievery, for tampering with seals or documents

It should not be a universal standalone skill.

Hole 8: Escaping Bonds

Escape Artist should be an activity rather than a skill.

Possible approaches:

Athletics: overpower or break restraints.
Acrobatics: contort out of them.
Thievery: manipulate locks or knots.
Crafting: understand the restraint’s construction.
Martial Arts: exploit a grapple or hold.

This is exactly the type of situation where your activity model is stronger than 3.5e’s standalone Escape Artist skill.

These should be Lore or Knowledge specialties, used with Diplomacy, Deception, Insight, or Leadership.

3. Craft, Lore, and Knowledge: Three Possible Models
Model A: Three Parent Skills with Specialties

Example:

Crafting — Blacksmithing
Crafting — Alchemy
Lore — Undead
Lore — Nobility
Knowledge — History
Knowledge — Engineering
Advantages
Very flexible.
Character sheet remains organized.
Specialties can be added without redesigning the whole system.
Excellent for high option density.
Disadvantages
The distinction between parent skill and specialty can be confusing.
It may create two layers of proficiency.
Players may ask whether “Crafting” itself can be rolled without a specialty.
Model B: Every Specialty Is an Independent Skill

Example:

Blacksmithing
Alchemy
Undead Lore
Nobility Lore
History
Engineering
Advantages
Maximum specificity.
Very clear investment.
Strong niche protection.
Closest to 3.5e’s Knowledge, Craft, and Profession model.
Disadvantages
Character sheets become crowded.
A player may have to choose among dozens of skills.
Many specialties will be campaign-dependent.
It risks recreating 3.5e’s skill bloat.
Model C: Broad Skills Plus Open-Ended Lore

Example:

Crafting is a broad skill.
Lore is individually named.
Knowledge is not a universal skill; formal knowledge is represented by Lore or a specific skill.

A character might have:

Crafting
Crafting Specialty: Blacksmithing
Lore: Undead
Lore: Imperial Law
Lore: Sailing Routes
Engineering
Anatomy and Healing
Advantages
Closest to PF2e’s most successful design.
Keeps Lore flexible and campaign-responsive.
Prevents a redundant generic Knowledge skill.
Supports both broad competence and narrow expertise.
Lets “Lore — X” become a true character-building vector.
Disadvantages
Requires clear GM guidance for selecting Lore fields.
“Lore” can become too broad if not bounded.
Crafting still needs a defined specialty procedure.
4. Recommended Result

I recommend Model C, with one adjustment:

Crafting should use defined specialties; Lore should be open-ended; Knowledge should not exist as one generic skill.

Final structure
Crafting

A defined skill with specialties.

Examples:

Craft — Blacksmithing
Craft — Bowyer
Craft — Shipwright
Craft — Alchemy
Craft — Engineering
Craft — Forgery
Craft — Tailoring

The specialty determines what can be made or repaired.

Lore

An open-ended, player-named knowledge skill.

Examples:

Lore — Undead
Lore — Elven Courts
Lore — Imperial Law
Lore — Sailing Routes
Lore — Goblin Tribes
Lore — Runes
Lore — Demons

Lore represents focused familiarity acquired through experience, culture, profession, travel, or study.

Formal knowledge

Do not create a generic Knowledge skill. Instead, use either:

a defined skill where the domain is important enough, or
a Lore specialty when it is campaign-specific.

Recommended formal skills:

Investigation
Languages and Scripts
Anatomy and Healing
Engineering
Cartography
Medicine, if separated from Anatomy and Healing
Magic-tradition skills, after spellcasting is revised

Examples:

Task	Recommended Skill
Remember a historical event	Lore — History
Identify a monster	Lore — Monsters or Lore — Undead
Understand a cipher	Languages and Scripts or Investigation
Decode magical runes	Lore — Runes, then a magic Tradition skill
Design a bridge	Craft — Engineering
Repair a bridge	Craft — Masonry or Engineering
Identify a poison	Anatomy and Healing or Lore — Poisons
Map an unknown coastline	Cartography
Navigate using that map	Sailing, Survival, or Cartography
Know a kingdom’s laws	Lore — Law
Argue a legal case	Diplomacy plus Lore — Law
