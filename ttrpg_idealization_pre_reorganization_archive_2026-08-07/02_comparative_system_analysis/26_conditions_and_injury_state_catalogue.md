# Conditions and Injury-State Catalogue

**Status:** Research catalogue; non-canonical.  
**Purpose:** Collect useful condition, affliction, injury, incapacitation, and recovery-state patterns from relevant systems before designing this project’s Conditions, Wounds, stabilization, and treatment procedures.

This document is evidence and vocabulary research. It does not establish a condition, effect, stacking rule, or recovery procedure for this project.

## 1. Reference Systems and Transferable Patterns

| System | Relevant condition pattern | Strong lesson for this project | Caution when adapting |
|---|---|---|---|
| D&D 3.5e SRD | A broad list of named states: sensory loss, mobility limits, fear, mental control, fatigue, poison/sickness, dying, and unconsciousness | A common vocabulary makes effects easy to find and adjudicate | Most entries rely on linear modifiers and ability-score penalties; translate their fictional effect, not their arithmetic |
| Pathfinder 2e | Conditions are grouped by function; many use values, action loss, detection states, and a linked dying/wounded structure | Conditions can be modular, concise, and explicit about duration and removal | Avoid importing its numeric values or condition-value bookkeeping wholesale |
| Shadow of the Demon Lord | Compact affliction list using Boons/Banes, movement, action restrictions, and Defense changes | Its affliction approach is compatible with a dice-pool game: fiction first, then a small procedural effect | Do not assume its exact boon/bane or Defense mechanics transfer |
| Savage Worlds | Shaken, Wounds, Fatigue, and Incapacitation form a short escalation ladder | Separate transient combat disruption from persistent injury and longer-term fatigue | Its universal penalties and soak procedure do not fit this project’s no-linear-modifier goal |
| Mythras / BRP | Hit locations plus Serious and Major Wounds distinguish local injury, incapacitation, and long recovery | Location should matter, especially for head, torso, arms, and legs; injury need not equal generic HP loss | Its local HP and detailed wound arithmetic are more granular than this project may want |
| Year Zero Engine | A depleted attribute causes a Broken state; physical collapse can trigger a critical-injury roll; recovery depends on the trauma type | A zero-resource state can cause incapacitation without negative-point arithmetic, while an injury table handles aftermath | Its attribute-damage model is not this project’s resource model |

## 2. Cross-System Condition Families

The systems repeatedly converge on the following functional families. A future project condition list should cover each required job once; it should not copy every synonym from every source.

| Family | Common reference names | Fictional job | Typical procedural levers for this project |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sensory loss | Blinded, Dazzled, Deafened | Sight or hearing is impaired or absent | Automatic failure for the affected perception; Banes; targeting or spellcasting restrictions |
| Position and exposure | Prone, Off-Guard/Flat-Footed, Knocked Down, Vulnerable | The creature is poorly positioned or unable to defend normally | Movement restrictions, defense permissions removed, attacker Boons, or limited actions |
| Restraint and movement denial | Grabbed, Grappled, Restrained, Pinned, Immobilized, Entangled, Bound | A force, creature, terrain, or object limits movement or manipulation | Prohibit movement, require Escape, restrict weapon/spell use, or impose Banes on specific actions |
| Action disruption | Dazed, Stunned, Slowed, Staggered, Incapacitated, Unconscious | The creature loses some or all ability to act | Remove or limit Actions, Reactions, movement, or available action categories; avoid generic penalties |
| Bodily control loss | Paralyzed, Petrified, Asleep, Helpless | The body cannot respond or the creature is defenseless | Prohibit physical actions; specify awareness, senses, and execution vulnerability |
| Fear and morale | Shaken, Frightened, Panicked, Fleeing, Cowering | Fear impairs judgment or compels withdrawal | Banes on relevant actions, movement compulsion, action restrictions, or morale-specific Permissions |
| Mental influence | Charmed, Fascinated, Confused, Compelled, Controlled, Dominated, Possessed | Attention, allegiance, or agency is altered | Restrict targets/actions, compel a defined behavior, or transfer action choice; define resistance and end triggers |
| Illness and toxins | Poisoned, Diseased, Sickened, Nauseated, Intoxicated | Internal biological threat impairs performance or causes harm | Fortitude resolution, periodic Damage Boxes or Conditions, recovery/treatment requirements |
| Exertion and depletion | Fatigued, Exhausted, Winded, Drained | The body or inner reserve is depleted | Resource-zero state, restricted expenditures, recovery-event restriction, or escalating condition states |
| Persistent harm | Bleeding, Burning, Corroded, Persistent Damage | Harm continues until stopped, treated, or resisted | End-of-turn/event Damage Boxes, a treatment Activity, or an escalating Wound; specify the end trigger |
| Anatomical injury | Concussion, Rib Trauma, Disabled Arm, Disabled Leg, Maimed | A specific body location is impaired | Restrict the linked actions, senses, equipment slot, movement, or defenses; use Wound severity and treatment |
| Death and recovery | Dying, Wounded, Stable, Doomed, Broken | The actor is near death, recovering from collapse, or at increased future risk | Incapacitation, stabilization procedure, injury roll, treatment, and death trigger |
| Visibility and detection | Concealed, Hidden, Invisible, Observed, Undetected | Parties have incomplete information about one another | Usually better handled by the spatial/perception procedure than as a universal persistent Condition |
| Object and equipment state | Broken, Damaged, Sundered, Disarmed, Armor Compromised | An item is impaired, unavailable, or altered | Durability loss, lost Trait/Permission, Damage Absorption reduction, repair requirement, or an item-specific Condition |
| Transformation and supernatural state | Cursed, Petrified, Polymorphed, Incorporeal, Silenced | The creature’s form, metaphysical state, or magical capability changes | Effect-specific Permissions/prohibitions; usually not a universal generic condition |

## 4. Useful Structural Patterns

### A. Escalation chains

Several systems gain clarity by using a small progression rather than many unrelated states:

```text
Fatigued → Exhausted → Incapacitated
Shaken → Frightened → Panicked / Fleeing
Grabbed → Restrained / Pinned
Wound → Severe Wound → Maimed
```

A project chain should only exist where each stage changes table procedure. Do not create a chain solely to add labels.

### B. Location plus severity

Mythras demonstrates that location makes Wounds meaningful, while Savage Worlds demonstrates that a small persistent-injury track is easy to run. A possible future project structure can combine:

```text
Location: head, chest, abdomen, off-arm, weapon arm, leg
+ Severity: minor, severe, critical, maimed
+ Condition: the actual play consequence
```

This is a design pattern only; no severity model is selected here.

### C. Condition effects without linear modifiers

When adapting a reference condition, prefer one or more of these levers:

1. **Permission/prohibition:** may not move; may only Escape; cannot cast spells with a Vocal requirement.
2. **Action or Reaction restriction:** lose a Reaction; only one Action category is available; cannot take a defensive maneuver.
3. **Movement restriction:** Speed reduced, cannot Stride, must Crawl, or must flee.
4. **Pool change:** a named Bane, Die Step-Down, or a specifically defined Boon to an opponent.
5. **Defense interaction:** specifies which defense cannot be used or which threat bypasses it.
6. **Resource interaction:** costs more, cannot recover, loses Damage Boxes over time, or cannot spend a resource.
7. **Treatment and end trigger:** ends after an Activity, medical treatment, rest event, save, or explicit spell.

Avoid importing generic `−2` or `−4` penalties from d20 systems.
