# Shared Spell-Slot Progression: Comparative Research Catalogue

**Research scope:** Pathfinder 2e, D&D 3.5e, 13th Age, Shadow of the Demon Lord, and D&D 5e.  
**Primary design question:** How should our shared spell-slot pool progress across character levels and modular paths without fractional caster levels or table-side arithmetic?

This document catalogs how the reference systems answer the seven OD-001A questions. It is comparative research, not yet canonical rules.

---

## 1. OD-001A Questions

1. How many spell slots does a character receive at each Character Level and High Fantasy Tier?
2. How do class and modular path levels contribute to a shared slot pool without fractional caster levels or table-side arithmetic?
3. Should full, partial, limited, and noncasting paths use distinct discrete progression tracks?
4. When a character multiclasses, how are slot contributions combined?
5. Does a character's highest Tradition Rank limit the highest slot they can use?
6. Do advanced paths increase slot quantity, maximum Spell Rank, or both?
7. How does Mythic advancement expand or transform the shared slot pool?

---

# 2. Pathfinder 2e

## 2.1 Basic model

PF2e uses spell **ranks**, not traditional spell levels for most rules presentation. A spellcaster receives a number of slots at each rank they can cast. Full spellcasters typically gain access to higher ranks as they level, with the exact class table determining the progression.

Cantrips are not prepared into ordinary slots and scale automatically with the character's spellcasting progression. Focus spells use a separate Focus Point pool and are not ordinary spell slots.

PF2e has both prepared and spontaneous casters, but both ultimately use rank-based spell slots. The preparation/repertoire distinction is separate from slot capacity.

## 2.2 Answers to the OD-001A questions

### 1. How many slots by level and tier?

PF2e answers this with class-specific tables. A full caster normally gains spell slots at every available spell rank and unlocks higher ranks at specific character levels. The exact number is usually a small fixed number per rank, with class features, spellcasting benefits, and special subsystems modifying the result.

PF2e does not use our six High Fantasy Tiers as a resource framework. Its progression is level-based and rank-based.

### 2. How do class/path levels contribute?

A class grants its own spellcasting progression. Archetype spellcasting feats grant additional spellcasting at a deliberately slower progression. PF2e does not generally ask the table to calculate a fractional universal caster level; the archetype feat has a defined progression.

### 3. Full, partial, limited, and noncasters?

Yes. PF2e distinguishes full spellcasting classes, bounded or partial spellcasting options, innate spellcasting, focus spellcasting, rituals, and noncasters with magical feats. These are represented through different class or archetype features rather than one universal contribution formula.

### 4. Multiclassing and shared slots?

Ordinary multiclass archetypes do not simply merge all casting into one universal pool. A character may have their primary class spellcasting and a separate archetype spellcasting progression. Each source has defined access and progression.

This is highly controlled but not the cleanest model for our desired universal pool.

### 5. Does proficiency limit highest spell rank?

Yes, in practice. A spellcasting proficiency rank and class progression determine access to higher-rank spells. A character cannot use a high-rank slot to cast a spell they cannot access by rank or proficiency. Proficiency also affects spell attack rolls, spell DCs, and other casting checks.

### 6. Do advanced paths increase slots, maximum rank, or both?

They can do either. Class progression unlocks higher spell ranks and provides slots. Feats and archetypes can add spells, focus spells, spell slots, or restricted access. The system generally separates proficiency, spell rank, and slot availability.

### 7. Mythic advancement?

The standard PF2e level progression ends at level 20. Mythic rules and high-level subsystems are separate from ordinary spell-slot progression. Mythic power is not simply represented by adding more ordinary spell slots.

## 2.3 Relevant lessons

- Spell rank and spell proficiency are separate but interact cleanly.
- Focus Points are a useful separate resource for special magical effects.
- Archetypes demonstrate how slower secondary casting can be represented without fractional caster levels.
- Separate preparation and spontaneous models can coexist with rank-based slots, but multiclassing becomes more complex when each source maintains a separate progression.
- Heightening is a deliberate operation; higher slots do not universally improve every spell automatically.

# 3. D&D 3.5e

## 3.1 Basic model

D&D 3.5e uses class-specific spell slots by spell level. Each class has its own progression table. A character's ability score may provide bonus spells, and some classes receive special domain, school, or specialization slots.

Caster Level is a separate class-derived value that scales spell duration, range, damage, dispelling, overcoming resistance, and other effects. Spell slots and Caster Level are related but not identical.

Different classes may use prepared spellcasting, spontaneous casting, or specialized systems. Clerics, Druids, and Wizards prepare spells; Sorcerers know a limited repertoire and cast spontaneously; Bards use a different spontaneous progression.

## 3.2 Answers to the OD-001A questions

### 1. How many slots by level and tier?

Every class has its own table. A full caster gradually gains access to spell levels 1 through 9. Partial casters gain lower levels more slowly or stop at a lower maximum. Bonus spells depend on the casting ability score. Specialist Wizards may receive an additional specialist spell slot at each spell level but lose access to prohibited schools.

D&D 3.5e has no High Fantasy Tier resource model. Its important breakpoints are class levels and spell levels.

### 2. How do class/path levels contribute?

They do not combine into one shared pool by default. Each class level contributes to that class's own spellcasting progression. Prestige classes can advance an existing spellcasting class, often by granting “+1 level of existing spellcasting class.”

Some prestige classes advance Caster Level, spells known, spells per day, and other class features together; others advance only selected parts.

### 3. Full, partial, limited, and noncasters?

Yes, but mostly through separate class tables:

- Full casters reach 9th-level spells.
- Partial casters reach lower maximum spell levels or progress later.
- Limited casters may have small spell lists or restricted daily slots.
- Noncasters have no ordinary spell-slot progression.
- Some classes use separate systems such as invocations, maneuvers, psionics, or supernatural abilities.

### 4. Multiclassing and shared slots?

Spell slots remain separate by class. A Cleric 3/Wizard 3 has Cleric slots and Wizard slots, each using the appropriate class's preparation and spell access. Caster Level is likewise tracked separately unless a prestige class advances one of the classes.

This produces high granularity but substantial bookkeeping.

### 5. Does proficiency limit highest spell rank?

A class's spellcasting ability and class level determine the highest spell level available. Ability scores also gate access to spells. Skill ranks do not normally determine spell level access; Caster Level is a class progression rather than a general skill rank.

The closest lesson for our system is the distinction between:

- Spell access.
- Daily slots.
- Caster-level scaling.
- Ability-score prerequisites.

### 6. Do advanced paths increase slots, maximum rank, or both?

Prestige classes may advance existing spellcasting, which generally improves spells per day, spells known, maximum spell level, and Caster Level as specified. Other prestige classes grant limited casting or unique spell-like abilities.

### 7. Mythic advancement?

The ordinary 3.5e system ends at level 20, with epic and epic spellcasting rules handled separately. Epic progression may increase caster level and spellcasting capacity, but it is not a simple continuation of ordinary class-slot tables.

## 3.3 Relevant lessons

- Caster Level is powerful for spell scaling, but separate caster levels become cumbersome in multiclassing.
- Prestige-class “advance existing spellcasting” is a useful model for modular paths.
- Independent class spell lists and pools maximize class identity but conflict with our shared Tradition model.
- Bonus slots based on ability score add complexity and should probably not be copied directly.
- Our proposed model can preserve 3.5e-style intrinsic spell scaling by using Tradition Competency instead of separate Caster Levels.

# 4. 13th Age

## 4.1 Basic model

13th Age does not use a universal Vancian spell-slot table in the same way as D&D 3.5e or 5e. Characters select powers, spells, talents, and class features according to class level. Abilities may be:

- At-will.
- Once per battle.
- Recharge-based.
- Daily.
- Flexible or escalation-based, depending on class.

Spellcasters often have a small number of highly impactful powers rather than a large grid of slots by spell level.

## 4.2 Answers to the OD-001A questions

### 1. How many slots by level and tier?

There is no universal slot-by-rank progression. The class determines how many powers or spells the character has at each level and how often each can be used.

13th Age's level bands are more important than a spell-slot table. Characters gain new powers and replace or improve older ones as they advance.

### 2. How do class/path levels contribute?

The class level directly determines available powers. The system does not normally calculate a universal caster level from fractional class contributions.

### 3. Full, partial, limited, and noncasters?

Class design handles this directly. A Wizard, Cleric, Sorcerer, Bard, or other magical class receives its own power cadence. Noncasters receive martial or utility powers using the same broad frequency categories.

### 4. Multiclassing and shared slots?

13th Age is primarily class-based. Optional multiclassing combines class features or power selections, but it does not provide a standard universal spell-slot pool comparable to 5e.

### 5. Does proficiency limit highest spell rank?

There is generally no separate spellcasting proficiency rank equivalent to PF2e. Class level and the specific power determine whether an ability is available. Some effects scale with level or use class-based attack and defense mechanics.

### 6. Do advanced paths increase slots, maximum rank, or both?

New levels provide new powers, improved damage, improved effects, and sometimes replacement or upgraded versions. The system usually does not express this as increasing spell slots and maximum spell rank separately.

### 7. Mythic advancement?

13th Age has high-level and epic-style play but does not use a standard Mythic spell-slot transformation in its ordinary core progression. Epic or campaign-specific rules are needed beyond the standard level range.

## 4.3 Relevant lessons

- Frequency categories can replace a large slot matrix for some abilities.
- The system is excellent at making every spell or power feel impactful.
- A shared pool could borrow the distinction between at-will, encounter, recharge, and daily effects, but our Vancian commitment means this should be treated as a secondary inspiration rather than the core model.
- 13th Age offers a useful approach to high-level spell replacement and scaling without simply adding more slots.

# 5. Shadow of the Demon Lord

## 5.1 Basic model

SotDL uses **Power** rather than a traditional caster level or spell-slot table. Power determines the highest rank of spell a character can cast. Paths grant traditions and individual spells.

Spells typically have their own number of castings or uses. A spell may be usable once, twice, or several times per day depending on its entry. The character does not spend a generic slot of the spell's rank in the D&D sense.

Traditions are modular and can be acquired through Novice, Expert, and Master paths. A character can combine traditions from multiple paths.

## 5.2 Answers to the OD-001A questions

### 1. How many slots by level and tier?

There is no shared spell-slot pool. Power increases with character advancement and determines the highest spell rank available. Each individual spell specifies its number of castings.

SotDL's path bands correspond conceptually to our modular path bands, but the system uses Power and per-spell castings rather than universal slots.

### 2. How do class/path levels contribute?

Paths grant Power increases, traditions, talents, and spells. There is no fractional caster-level calculation. A character's accumulated Power is a bounded progression supplied by ancestry, novice/expert/master paths, and level advancement.

### 3. Full, partial, limited, and noncasters?

Yes, through path design. A caster-focused path grants more Power, traditions, and spells. A martial path may grant no Power or only a limited magical feature. Hybrid paths grant smaller or more restricted access.

### 4. Multiclassing and shared slots?

There are no shared slots to combine. A character accumulates Power, traditions, and spells from their paths. Multiple paths can broaden the character's magical repertoire, while the Power value controls the highest spell rank.

### 5. Does proficiency limit highest spell rank?

Power determines the highest spell rank. There is no separate Tradition Competency Rank equivalent to our skill model. Traditions determine spell access; Power determines spell rank capacity.

This is the clearest precedent for separating:

- Tradition access.
- Spell-rank capacity.
- Individual spell acquisition.

### 6. Do advanced paths increase slots, maximum rank, or both?

Advanced paths primarily increase Power, grant higher-rank spells, add traditions, and provide talents. They do not increase a universal slot pool because slots are not the core resource model.

### 7. Mythic advancement?

SotDL's standard progression is ten levels and does not have our six-tier Mythic structure. Beyond-standard advancement requires separate campaign or supplement rules.

## 5.3 Relevant lessons

- Power is a clean bounded measure of maximum spell rank.
- Individual per-spell castings give designers precise control over strong effects.
- Modular paths can grant access without creating separate class spell lists.
- The system is an excellent model for Tradition access but not a direct model for our committed shared Vancian slots.
- We can borrow its discrete full/partial/limited path contribution concept without copying its per-spell-use model.

# 6. D&D 5e

## 6.1 Basic model

D&D 5e uses a shared spell-slot progression for multiclass characters with ordinary spellcasting. Each class still has its own spell list and preparation or known-spell rules, but spell slots are combined through a multiclass spellcaster-level table.

Full casters contribute one level per class level. Half-casters and third-casters contribute fractions according to class rules, usually rounded down. Warlock Pact Magic is separate and does not combine normally with ordinary spellcasting slots.

Cantrips are at-will and scale primarily from total character level. Higher-level slots can be used to cast lower-level spells, and many spells improve when cast with a higher slot.

## 6.2 Answers to the OD-001A questions

### 1. How many slots by level and tier?

The standard multiclass spellcaster table provides a shared number of slots by spell level based on the combined spellcaster level. Full casters progress from 1st-level slots at low level to 9th-level slots at high level. Slot quantity increases at defined character-level breakpoints.

D&D 5e has no formal High Fantasy Tier resource structure, though its spell-level breakpoints create practical tiers of play.

### 2. How do class/path levels contribute?

Class levels contribute to a combined spellcaster level:

- Full-caster levels contribute fully.
- Half-caster levels contribute partially.
- Third-caster levels contribute more slowly.
- The final result is checked against one shared slot table.

This is mathematically compact in the book but requires fractional progression and rounding rules during character creation.

### 3. Full, partial, limited, and noncasters?

Yes. Full, half, and third casters use different contribution rates. Warlock Pact Magic is a distinct casting system. Racial spells, feats, and innate magic operate outside the ordinary class slot progression.

### 4. Multiclassing and shared slots?

Ordinary spellcaster levels combine into one shared slot pool. However, spells known and prepared remain class-specific. A multiclass Wizard/Cleric has one shared pool but separately tracks Wizard and Cleric spell access and preparation.

This is the closest direct precedent for our desired shared-slot model, but our proposal goes further by making Tradition lists shared rather than class-owned.

### 5. Does proficiency limit highest spell rank?

Spellcasting class level determines which spell levels the character can learn or prepare. Proficiency bonus affects some spell attacks and class features but does not independently determine spell-slot rank.

D&D 5e's caster-level function is split between:

- Class spellcasting progression.
- Spell-slot table.
- Ability score.
- Proficiency bonus.
- Character level for cantrip scaling.

### 6. Do advanced paths increase slots, maximum rank, or both?

Multiclass spellcaster progression increases both as the combined spellcaster level rises. Subclasses generally modify spell access and class features rather than creating a new universal slot table. Feats may add spells or limited casting without advancing ordinary spellcaster level.

### 7. Mythic advancement?

The standard game ends at level 20 and normally caps ordinary spell slots at 9th level. Epic boons and high-level features provide additional power but do not normally create 10th-level spell slots in the core progression.

## 6.3 Relevant lessons

- One shared slot pool is compatible with different classes, but class-owned spell lists create the exact shared-Tradition ambiguity we are removing.
- The combined spellcaster-level table is familiar but uses fractions and rounding.
- Separate Pact Magic demonstrates that not every supernatural resource must join the shared slot pool.
- Cantrips scaling by character level is a useful precedent, though our design currently favors Essence for 0th-rank effects.
- D&D 5e supports shared slots but still has class-specific preparation and acquisition, whereas our system will unify those around Traditions plus class acquisition features.

# 8. Design Conclusions for Our System

The reference systems suggest that our system should preserve the following:

1. **PF2e:** Separate spell rank from casting proficiency.
2. **D&D 3.5e:** Let casting competence scale the intrinsic effect of a spell.
3. **13th Age:** Keep individual magical effects impactful and use discrete advancement breakpoints.
4. **SotDL:** Let modular paths grant Traditions and use discrete path-based magical progression.
5. **D&D 5e:** Use one shared slot pool for multiclass spellcasters.

The main feature to avoid is D&D 5e's fractional multiclass caster-level arithmetic. Our implementation should use discrete path tags or contribution bands such as:

- Full Casting.
- Partial Casting.
- Limited Casting.
- No Casting.

The final shared-slot table should convert those path contributions into a discrete result at level-up rather than requiring players to add fractions at the table.

# 9. Open Questions After Comparative Research

The comparative survey does not yet answer our design choices. It narrows them to:

- Should Tradition Competency alone gate the highest Spell Rank, or must a separate class/path spell-capacity track also gate it?
- Should slot quantity be determined by total Character Level, cumulative casting-path bands, or a discrete full/partial/limited track?
- Should higher-rank slots ever heighten lower-rank spells, or should our D&D 3.5e-style intrinsic scaling remain primary?
- Should each spell have a fixed slot cost equal to its Spell Rank, or can specific spells have exceptional slot costs?
- Should 0th-rank spells be entirely Essence-based, entirely at-will, or divided into both categories?
- How much spell-slot growth should occur at Levels 1, 5, 9, 13, 17, and Mythic advancement?
